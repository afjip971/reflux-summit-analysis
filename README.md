## The Actual Problem with Treating Acid Reflux

Most conventional approaches to acid reflux treat the symptom — too much acid — without asking *why* acid is going where it shouldn't.

Here's what experts featured at the summit point out: reflux isn't always a production problem. Low stomach acid, SIBO (small intestinal bacterial overgrowth), H. pylori infection, a weak lower esophageal sphincter, a hiatal hernia, or even poor vagal tone can all trigger reflux-like symptoms. PPIs (proton pump inhibitors) can mask these issues for years while the underlying condition quietly worsens.

According to one review published in *BMC Primary Care* cited on the Reflux Summit blog, even something as simple as elevating the head of your bed can reduce acid exposure time by a measurable amount — but most people aren't told this, or aren't told *how* to do it properly.

The summit exists to close that gap.

**Q: What's the difference between GERD and LPR?**
A: GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease) causes classic symptoms: heartburn, regurgitation, chest discomfort.
